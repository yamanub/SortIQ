What this is

SortIQ is the brain of an automatic brass-sorting machine. It looks at the head of a fired case through a camera, reads the headstamp (the maker's marking, like WIN or FC), and drops the case into the matching bin. Anything it isn't sure about goes to the UNMATCHED bin — the machine would rather make you re-run a case than put it in the wrong bin.

Sorting is by headstamp only: one bin per headstamp, plus unmatched. SortIQ does not detect crimped primer pockets.

How a case gets sorted

1 · Photo One photo of the case head, framed by the live crop preview.
↓
2 · Quality gate The photo must be sharp enough. A blurry photo is rejected before the AI ever sees it — better no guess than a bad guess.
↓
3 · Embedding match A neural network turns the headstamp crop into a compact fingerprint vector, then compares it against a gallery of exemplar photos — a handful of representative images per headstamp, picked automatically for coverage (you can pin or exclude any on the Dataset page). The closest class wins, with a similarity score.
↓
4 · Three honesty gates The match must clear that class's own bar (each class earns its bar from how tightly its exemplars agree), and it must be clearly ahead of the runner-up — a near-tie between look-alikes is rejected as "too close to call" rather than coin-flipped. The case is also read at four rotations; disagreement is logged as a warning sign.
↓
5 · Bin All gates pass → the stamp's bin. Anything less → UNMATCHED, with a plain-language reason ("Too close to call — SPEER 91% vs BLAZER 89%").

Why matching beats guessing

A plain classifier has to pick one of the classes it was trained on — a stamp it has never seen still comes out as somebody's name, often confidently. The embedding+gallery design is open-set: a stranger doesn't resemble anyone's exemplars strongly enough to clear the gates, so it lands in UNMATCHED instead of a bin. And adding a new headstamp needs no retraining — file 3+ photos and the gallery starts matching it immediately.

The rejection philosophy

  • Rejected (sent to UNMATCHED): costs you one re-run of that case. Cheap.
  • Misfiled (wrong bin): silently contaminates a bin. Expensive.

Every threshold exists to trade reject-rate for misfile-rate. When in doubt, the machine rejects. Your target is a misfile rate of zero at whatever reject rate that costs.

Growing the dataset — doing it right

Headstamps and photos are managed on the Collect and Dataset tabs; the Sort page's bin cards assign each physical bin its headstamps. Exactly one bin is UNMATCHED — the catch-all for rejects; stamps without a bin of their own ride it too, unless you assign an optional OVERFLOW bin to catch them instead. (On the servo carousel both tokens sit on the open Bin 9 pocket by default and can be moved onto a gated bin; the × on the moved pill sends one back.) A class is live in the gallery — and therefore sortable — from 3 photos; it joins the periodic training pass (which sharpens the whole network) at 10+.

Rule 1 — photograph like the machine will see

  • Fix the camera, the case position, and the lighting, and never touch them again mid-dataset.
  • Zoom and pan onto the headstamp first: push the Zoom slider in Collect › Camera setup up until the headstamp covers the majority of the frame — a small stamp lost in a wide shot starves the model of pixels — then use Pan left/right and Pan up/down to re-center the primer (pan needs Zoom above 1× to have anything to work with).
  • Judge quality in the live crop preview: if you can read the stamp easily, the model can learn it. Dark, etched-looking letters on bright metal beat washed-out glare every time.
  • Set the light ring brightness so the stamped letters read as crisp dark marks — not blown out by glare, not lost in shadow. Diffuse the light for reflective nickel cases — point light creates glare that burns out exactly the letters you need.

Rule 2 — quantity targets

Class sizeWhat it means
1–2 photosthe class exists, waiting for siblings ("needs 3+ to enter the gallery")
3–9 photoslive in the gallery — sortable today, skipped by the training pass
10–300full citizen: gallery + training
300+saves are skipped only when pixel-verified as the same physical case already filed (matching scratch pattern at some rotation — re-run brass); distinct cases always file, however alike the stamp looks
500+well-fed: batch filing switches from volume to variety — File novel keeps only photos showing the class a genuinely new look and skips routine near-identicals (File all overrides). Hard cases (the "Looks like X" and unknown piles) always file in full — they teach the most.

The Dataset and Train pages draw these tiers as bars filling toward 500 (tick marks at the 10-image training gate and the 300-image duplicate gate): red under 10, brass 10–299, amber 300+, green 500+ — sort by count and the red tail is your re-photo shopping list.

Two ways to collect: the chained capture loop (Collect tab — feed, photograph, one keypress per case, with the model pre-selecting labels) and batch capture (run the whole hopper at machine speed, then confirm grouped cards — whole groups file with one click, and any case can be removed from its group first). For a hopper that is all one headstamp, set the hopper to One class: the machine files the cases that look like that class and holds the rest for you to check. Variety beats volume — many different physical cases (worn, tarnished, shiny) beat many shots of one case.

Rule 3 — unknown is a state, not a category

When a stamp you don't recognize shows up (in a reject review or the capture flow), choose Set aside — identify later. Set-aside photos land in the tray at the top of the Dataset page, clustered by similarity so five copies of the same mystery stamp arrive as one group. Name the group (the community headstamp list below is the naming cheat sheet) and every photo files at once — the class is created and, at 3+ photos, starts sorting immediately. The tray exists to be emptied.

Class structure — split for the model, family for the bin

The matcher identifies by look, not brand. When one headstamp name covers visibly different dies (FC plain / dots / diamonds, WIN plain / numbers / +P), give each look its own class — a variant hiding inside its parent matches at coin-flip margins, while a split class matches decisively. Then group the variants into a family (Dataset page) and assign the family to a bin: they sort together physically, and a close call between two family members headed to the same bin is accepted instead of rejected — hesitating between siblings costs nothing. Keep classes honest with the mislabel scan (seconds, whole dataset) and re-scan after fixes. The full doctrine: docs/TRAINING_GUIDE.md.

Training

  • The gallery handles day-to-day growth with no training at all — new classes, new photos, pins and exclusions all take effect at the next Rebuild gallery — seconds, on either machine (unchanged photos reuse their stored vectors; only a fresh model pays full price once).
  • A training run needs 100+ crops across the whole dataset to start at all — a dataset-wide floor, separate from any single class's 10-image training threshold. Below it, Start training refuses with an error naming the crop count; collect more brass or pull more of the dataset from the machine first.
  • A full retraining of the embedding network sharpens everything — worth doing after the dataset has grown or shifted substantially. On the machine, Train models… (Train tab) opens a window that drives the trainer on the PC you're browsing from — sync, device choice, progress, result, install, all without leaving this page. Pick GPU (about an hour) or CPU (overnight — the trainer stays usable) and press Start. The result is staged as a candidate with its bench numbers; nothing replaces the live model until you press Install, which archives the current generation (restorable from the Train page) and pushes the new model + gallery pair to the machine. If the GPU fails, the app asks whether to retry it or train on CPU — it never switches by itself.
  • After moving/deleting images or changing the crop geometry: Rebuild crops, then Rebuild gallery.

The gates

GateWhat it doesWhere it comes from
Sharpness floorMinimum photo crispness; catches bumped cameras and mid-drop shotsrig-specific, set in the profile
Class barThe match must be at least this similar to the class's exemplarsearned per class from how tightly its exemplars agree (floor 85%); the Sort page's Acceptance slider shifts every bar together — 85% is neutral, higher is stricter
Runner-up marginThe winner must clearly beat the second-best class — near-ties reject as "too close to call"fixed small gap (3%)

The bars tune themselves from the gallery — the practical lever is the dataset. Lots of correct-but-rejected cases of one stamp → more photos of it (especially the looks it rejects on), then rebuild the gallery. A misfiled case → find it in the run report, refile it to the right class, and check that class for mislabeled images (Scan for mislabels).

Testing & validation

  • Test tab: classify from the live camera, an upload, or a synthetic case. Every gate shows its measured value, the bars show the closest gallery matches (winner and runner-up with their similarities), and the Decision card names the destination bin. A reject explains itself in plain language.
  • Test → Add to training data: when the model gets one wrong, correct the label and save it — the images it struggles with are the most valuable training data there is. The Test tab is also the mislabel detective: if a known case matches the wrong class, the runner-up trail usually leads to a mislabeled image sitting in that class's folder.
  • Sort tab: full sorting runs (simulator or hardware) with a live feed, per-bin counts, throughput, and a ground-truth scorecard in sim mode: correct / rejected / misfiled. Misfiled is the number to drive to zero. Every case's photo is kept for the run report, so any card can be reassigned or filed into the dataset afterwards.

Moving to the Pi

Getting the Pi online

  1. First boot — set Wi-Fi before flashing. In the Raspberry Pi Imager, use the settings gear when writing the SD card: set the hostname (pisortiq), enable SSH, and enter your Wi-Fi SSID and password. The Pi joins your network on its very first boot — no monitor or keyboard needed. Then browse to http://pisortiq.local:5000 — or, from the trainer's Train page, click Find machine… to scan the network (works even when .local names don't resolve).
  2. Ethernet always works — plug in and the Pi gets an address via DHCP alongside (or instead of) Wi-Fi.
  3. Changing Wi-Fi later: click the network readout in the page header (top right) — it shows every interface with its address, scans for nearby networks, and joins or forgets them (it drives NetworkManager, standard on Raspberry Pi OS). Joining a new network drops the old one, so do it while connected over ethernet when you can.
  4. No known network in range: the Pi raises its own hotspot, SortIQ-<hostname> (password sortbrass). Join it, open http://10.42.0.1:5000, and use the network dialog to put the machine on your Wi-Fi; the hotspot stands down once it joins.

More than one machine

Every machine answers at <its-hostname>.local:5000 — hostnames are set at flash time and must be unique (two Pis sharing one makes the name flip between them). Click the name in the page header to label each machine ("Line 1"); the label shows in the header, tab title, and the trainer's Find machine… list. The load-bearing rule: give each machine's model a unique name (9mm/Master, 9mm/Secondary) — the trainer files its dataset mirrors by caliber/model, and it will refuse to pull two different machines into one mirror. Full fleet guidance: docs/USER_MANUAL.md.

Installing the app

One command from your workstation does the whole install (rsync + venv + inference stack + a boot service), and re-running it later ships updates without touching the Pi's settings or collected images: 

tools/pi_deploy.sh          # defaults to pisortiq@pisortiq.local

The full walkthrough — flashing, deploying, and the first-connect bring-up checklist — is in docs/PI_SETUP.md. Models and config come along unchanged (nothing is retrained), and training stays on the PC: Train models… on the machine's Train tab syncs the dataset to the trainer, trains there, and installs the result back.

The touchscreen

An optional 7" HDMI touchscreen (1024 × 600) on the machine's post runs the everyday work from the machine itself. Its parts, printed mount and wiring are in hardware/touchscreen in the GitHub repo: HDMI to the Pi's HDMI 0 port (next to the USB-C power socket), and one micro-USB lead to a Pi USB port for touch and power. Plug it in before running tools/pi_deploy.sh and the installer sets it up: a kiosk that opens the touchscreen page at every boot, the screen's mode, and a hidden mouse pointer. Adding one to a machine that is already set up: plug it in and run the installer again. No driver or config.txt changes are needed.

  • Sort: the bins with live counts, the last case, Start sorting, Pause and Stop. Press and hold a bin to change its classes. The case feeder's strip runs the feeder (pick a speed, Go / Stop).
  • Capture: Single (feed one, pick the class, save) or Batch, a whole hopper as a mixed lot or one class.
  • Review: the newest run's rejects and sorted cases, filed into the dataset with a tap.
  • Stats: the thinnest classes, the last run, Rebuild gallery and Copy to USB.
  • Machine: home, test feed, the sort doors, relay, board power and the case feeder.
  • System (the gear): board power, screen sleep, machine info, restart or shut down the Pi, and the Wi-Fi network.

The screen sleeps after the idle time set on the System page, never during a run, and the tap that wakes it presses nothing. Pause, a tube jam or a feeder jam stops the run in place and says on the screen what to clear. When brass stops arriving, a countdown asks whether a case is stuck before the run finishes.

The Pico machines — sort arm and carousel

The SKR Pico board runs two SortIQ machine designs under the same serial protocol the app has always spoken, so one app drives all four hardware configurations. The Machine tab's Hardware card detects which one is connected from the board's version reply — on every connect and again after any reboot — and shows only that hardware's settings.

Building one: the carousel's renders, parts list and screw counts are in hardware/carousel in the GitHub repo, and the controller case that holds the Pi and the SKR Pico is in hardware/pico-controller. The optional touchscreen is in hardware/touchscreen (The touchscreen).

Sort arm wiring: feed motor on X (step 11 / dir 10 / en 12), sort arm on Y (step 6 / dir 5 / en 7), feed homing opto on X-STOP (GPIO 4), sort homing opto on Y-STOP (GPIO 3), brass sensor on WD-DET (GPIO 22). Pull both the X and Y DIAG jumpers — the homing optos share the endstop inputs. The carousel uses the same harness with the Y motor and Y-STOP simply left empty (and, with a case feeder, its motor on E and beam on Z-STOP), so one loom fits either machine. The full pin tables are in the firmware READMEs (firmware/SortIQ_Pico, firmware/SortIQ_PicoServo).

Building one takes five steps, and nothing needs setting up by hand afterwards (the full guide is docs/PICO_MACHINE_SETUP.md):

  1. Print the parts from hardware/carousel and hardware/pico-controller.
  2. Build and wire it from the wiring drawings and the Pi header table below.
  3. Set up the Pi: Raspberry Pi OS Lite with the Imager, then tools/pi_deploy.sh from your computer. The installer also sets the Pi up for the SKR Pico (header UART, LED ring PWM, I²C, logs), and the touchscreen if one is plugged in, and reboots it.
  4. Flash the SKR Pico with the image that ships in the repo: BOOT jumper on, board powered, ~/SortIQ/tools/flash_pico.sh carousel (or arm) on the Pi, then take the jumper off.
  5. Power it up. The app finds the board on the header UART, recognises which machine it is, and applies that build's reference settings, including the carousel's 9-slot layout with the open pocket.

Updating the board's firmware needs no jumper: ~/SortIQ/tools/flash_pico.sh asks the board to enter its bootloader over the link, flashes the image for the build this machine runs, and the app reconnects and sends every saved setting again. The board's logic runs off 24 V, not USB, so it must be powered to flash.

Moving a machine to another Pi: run the installer against the new Pi, then copy config.json (machine and camera tuning, door pulses, relay flag, case feeder settings, screen sleep) and the profile's model.json (bins, families, imaging, floors) across and restart the app. Wheel steps and the feed homing offset also live on the board itself (EEPROM).

Case sensor gotchas. Remove the board's NPN jumper for a PNP sensor such as the LJ8A3 -Z/BY; fit it only for an NPN -Z/BX. And the sensor plugs into WD-DET, not the identical-looking Laser header next to it: the Laser header's signal pin is FAN3's output (IO20), so a sensor there lights up but never reaches GPIO 22.

Two supplies never touch the Pi's header. The MOSFET module's power comes from the SKR Pico's E0-STOP header (5 V and GND); only its trigger pair goes to the Pi.
