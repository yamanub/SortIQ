SKR Pico Sorter Wiring

A second sorter built on a BigTreeTech SKR Pico and a Raspberry Pi 4 — the test bed for the controller that may replace the CS7.2 board. Every connection in bench order; pin numbers are the board's published GPIO map and the firmware port will assume exactly these.

The one rule of this board: the RP2040's inputs take 3.3 V, never 5 V. Every signal that arrives at a GPIO must be 3.3 V logic — the optical sensors run from 3.3 V, the prox goes into the WD-DET header, whose own conditioning circuit brings its 12/24 V output down to logic level — and nothing from the 5 V or 12 V rails touches an input directly.

Overview

Wire it in this order

  1. Nothing powered. Set the jumpers first: the DIAG jumper on the X and Y driver positions (so StallGuard reaches the board), drivers in UART mode (the Pico's default — its 2209s are soldered on).
  2. Motors. Feed stepper to X, sort stepper to Y. Same 4-wire plugs as today; if a motor runs backwards later, that's a firmware direction flag, not a rewire.
  3. Homing sensors to the endstop headers, powered from 3.3 V (see the meter check below before plugging in).
  4. Prox straight into WD-DET (brown → VIN, blue → GND, black → P_S). Two jumpers set it up: fit the Proximity Switch jumper (feeds the header VIN) and remove the NPN jumper — the LJ8A3-…-Z/BY is PNP.
  5. LED ring, fan, solenoid on the MOSFET outputs.
  6. Pi link: three wires from the Raspberry Pi header to the Pi's GPIO header (TX→15, RX→14, GND→GND). Leave the header's 5 V pin off for a Pi 5.
  7. 12 V to VIN/GND. Power up and run the checks.

The two small circuits

Prox on WD-DET, no extra parts

prox brown  (+V)  → WD-DET 12/24V
prox blue   (0 V) → WD-DET GND
prox black  (out) → WD-DET P_S   ← conditioned on-board → GPIO 22

jumper "Proximity Switch"  FITTED   (header Vcc = VIN)
jumper "NPN"               REMOVED  (LJ8A3-…-Z/BY is PNP)

The board does what the opto module would have: the P_S line goes through a divider to the RP2040, so the sensor's 12/24 V output never reaches a GPIO. A PNP sensor pulls the line high on brass; the firmware ships with sensor type 1 for exactly that — if the meter says otherwise, feedsensortype:0 flips it, no rewire.

WS2812 ring on the SERVOS header

SERVOS header 5 V    → ring 5V / VCC
SERVOS header signal → ring DIN  (data IN, not OUT)
SERVOS header GND    → ring GND

Full white on 12 pixels is ~720 mA from the 5 V rail — fine for the board's regulator, but give the servos their own 5 V supply through the PCA9685's V+ input so a dozen twitching servos never share a rail with the camera light. Pick one color mix for imaging and keep it fixed, the same rule as the stock ring.

Meter checks before the first run

  • Endstop header power pin: with the board powered and nothing in the headers, meter the V pin on E0-STOP/Z-STOP. 3.3 V → power the optical modules from it. 5 V → don't; feed them from a 3.3 V pin instead.
  • Opto output: OUT ≈ 3.3 V with no case, ≈ 0 V with brass in front (or the reverse — just note which).
  • DIAG at rest: X-STOP and Y-STOP read low with the motors idle.
  • No 5 V on any signal pin — the only 5 V wire on the board goes to the ring.
  • Ring lights at the firmware's level on the SERVOS header (no flicker, correct color); fan spins on FAN2.

The spares are where phase 2 lands: the gated-wheel's PCA9685 servo driver on the reserved I²C pair (GPIO 20 SDA / 25 SCL — the spare FAN3 pin and Z-STOP, which the sort arm gives up), with the servos powered from their own 5 V through the PCA9685's V+; plus a pocket-empty sensor on a thermistor input.

Motor power comes and goes while the board runs. With the Pi always on and the 12 V switched at will, the firmware treats motor power as a state: it notices when the drivers appear or vanish (PWR-DET, or the drivers answering on UART), re-applies current / microstep / StallGuard settings every time power returns, and tells SortIQ "motor power off" so the Machine page says so instead of the first feed failing as a mystery jam.

GPIO numbers from BigTreeTech's published SKR Pico v1.0 pin map. Header voltages vary by board revision — hence the meter checks. The firmware is a port of the SortIQ CS7.2 fork to this board with the same serial protocol, so nothing on the Pi changes.
